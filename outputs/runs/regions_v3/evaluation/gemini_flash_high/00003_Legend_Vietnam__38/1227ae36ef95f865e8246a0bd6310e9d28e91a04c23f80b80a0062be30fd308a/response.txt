      "text": "This is one of the most told stories from Vietnamese tradition. It unsurprisingly conforms to the feminist notion that women are portrayed in a negative light. The virtuous female character has no agency. She does what her stepmother tells her to without objection. Her contribution to the household involves cleaning, cooking, and everything related to the woman’s realm. In the absence of a father figure, her stepmother takes on the male role and confines her to the kitchen. Tam helplessly surrenders every time a problem arises. She resorts to relying on the Goddess for assistance. The Goddess is a significant figure in Buddhist teachings. She protects communities that honor her, similar to Patron Saints in the Christian faith. She represents the only positive female figure in the entire narrative. Her presence, although encouraging and helpful to Tam, does not necessarily contribute to a positive portrayal of women as a whole. She represents a powerful entity that possesses abilities beyond that of an average woman. Her existence sets impossible standards for women to live up to. The antagonist of this story is the wicked stepmother. She embodies another one of the female archetypes commonly seen in folk narratives, the bad or evil mother. Her malicious nature gives her agency to plot against Tam. The underlining message is that good women listen and obey. Bad women do as they please and face terrible fates."
